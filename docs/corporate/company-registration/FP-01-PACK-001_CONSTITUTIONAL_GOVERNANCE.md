## Part A — Memorandum of Association: fresh drafting brief

### Main object

To undertake, contract for, manage, supervise, construct, repair, renovate, fit out and complete physical civil, structural, architectural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing and infrastructure works; to procure labour, materials, plant and specialist services for such works; and to provide physical construction execution, site management, quality, safety, measurement, certification and handover services.

### Incidental objects

Solely to support the main object, the Company may acquire or lease execution equipment and premises, engage execution personnel and subcontractors, tender for works, maintain project records, obtain lawful registrations and insurance, open banking facilities, and purchase arm's-length professional or digital outputs required for a physical project.

### Express exclusion and IP firewall

The Company shall not develop, own, commercialise, license or operate software, SaaS, digital platforms, source code, algorithms, models, knowledge bases, datasets, digital product specifications, domains, or the Ferrum OS ecosystem or brand. Purchase or licensed use of a final digital output for a physical execution engagement does not transfer the underlying technology, data, brand, goodwill or intellectual property. Any future licence must be separately written, priced at arm's length, approved under applicable related-party rules, and must not amend this object by implication.

The practicing professional must settle the final NIC-aligned object wording and confirm it is accepted by the Registrar before filing.

### 2. Execution Matters

Execution Matters are day-to-day physical delivery decisions for an accepted construction project: site sequencing; deployment of approved labour, plant and materials; subcontractor coordination; approved method statements; measurement; quality and safety controls; client/site communications; and expenditure within the approved project budget and limits in Schedule 1. 3. constitutional amendments, merger, acquisition, restructuring, insolvency or sale of material assets;
4. borrowing, guarantees, security, banking mandates or expenditure outside approved limits;
5. appointment/removal and remuneration of directors, key personnel, auditors or advisers;
6. any use, licence, acquisition, creation or disposal of technology, data, intellectual property, brand, domain or goodwill;
7. related-party arrangements, including purchases from Sharief or any Ferrum OS business;
